# Review Comments

Help the author judge and address a specific issue. Don't invent or upgrade findings to improve the prose.

- Anchor feedback to the relevant passage, behaviour, or location. Describe the issue and its consequence rather than giving a general verdict such as “unclear” or “needs work”.
- Explain why the issue matters when the consequence is not obvious. Use a concrete reader misunderstanding or failure case when it clarifies the concern.
- Distinguish a required correction, a suggestion, and a question. Use the review's existing conventions rather than imposing a new severity system.
- State a verified issue directly. Ask a genuine question when information is missing; avoid disguising a demand as “Could we perhaps…?”
- Suggest a direction or replacement when useful, while leaving room for a better solution. Do not prescribe a rewrite when the problem can be stated clearly and the author is better placed to solve it.
- Address the work rather than the author's competence or motives. When giving praise, name what works and why it is worth keeping.
- Consolidate repeated symptoms into one comment when the same correction resolves them. Keep independent issues separate so they can be discussed and resolved individually.
- When replying, say what changed, why it remains as written, or what information is needed. Put durable explanations in the underlying work when future readers will need them.

## Example

Vague: “This could be clearer. Maybe reword?”

Actionable: “This says the rollout starts on Monday, but the timeline below says Wednesday. Which date is correct? Please align both so the support team knows when to prepare.”

Before returning, check that the author can locate the issue, understand its significance, and tell whether a response or change is expected.
