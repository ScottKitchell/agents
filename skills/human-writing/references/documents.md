# Documents

Choose the structure by why someone will read the document. A file type or the label “doc” does not determine its purpose.

| Reader's task | Useful structure |
| --- | --- |
| Decide | Recommendation, evidence, material alternatives and trade-offs, decision needed |
| Complete a task | Prerequisites, ordered steps, expected result, relevant recovery guidance |
| Understand | Concrete question or example, explanation built on what the reader knows, implications |
| Look something up | Predictable headings, precise definitions, complete entries within the stated scope |
| Follow an argument or story | An opening that establishes interest and direction, development, an earned ending |

These are choices, not section templates.

- Make the title and opening establish the subject and scope. For a proposal, surface the recommendation early; for an article, a concrete opening may do more work than a summary.
- Build the outline around the reader's questions. Use headings that distinguish the actual subjects rather than generic labels such as “Overview” and “Details”.
- Give an unfamiliar reader the context needed at the point of use. Define important terms once and use them consistently.
- Keep evidence near the claim it supports. Separate observed results, interpretations, and recommendations; preserve attribution when condensing sources.
- Move optional depth to a later section or linked source when that improves the main reading path. Keep qualifications beside the claims they constrain.
- Make durable documents understandable without the accompanying chat. Replace ambiguous references such as “the current approach” or “next week” when their meaning would decay.
- For an edit, preserve the document's scope and useful structure. Do not turn a sentence correction into a rewrite.

## Example

For the same subject, “Set up scheduled exports” needs prerequisites, steps, and a way to check success. “Why scheduled exports arrive late” needs a causal explanation. Do not force both into the same outline.

Before returning, skim the title and headings alone, then check that a reader can follow the main path without opening every link.
