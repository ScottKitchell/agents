# Overviews

Help someone orient themselves to a larger body of work and decide where to look next.

- Establish what the overview covers and what changed or matters. Assume the reader has not followed the working conversation.
- Select details by consequence for this audience. Explain the problem, outcome, or significance before implementation detail unless the reader's task requires otherwise.
- Summarise the final state. Omit investigation history and abandoned approaches unless they explain a material choice.
- Use a concrete example when an abstract description leaves the effect unclear. Keep examples faithful to the source; distinguish actual failures from hypothetical ones.
- Preserve consequential limits, unresolved questions, and dependencies. Distinguish completed work from plans and recommendations.
- For a handover, include the current state, the next action, and where to find what is needed to continue. Include owners or dates only when established.
- Link to supporting detail with labels that tell the reader what they will find. Make the essential conclusion understandable without following the links.
- Scale the overview to the work. A small change may need two sentences; avoid sections that add no information.

## Example

Activity inventory: “Updated the parser, changed the cache, and added validation.”

Useful orientation: “Imports now reject rows with missing account IDs before saving. Previously, those rows appeared to import successfully but disappeared from account views.”

Before returning, check that the overview accurately represents the source and gives the reader a useful next place to look, if one is needed.
