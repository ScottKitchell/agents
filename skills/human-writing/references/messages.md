# Messages

Assume the recipient reads this hours later, outside the current conversation.

- Distinguish a request, an update, and an invitation to discuss. Make the expected response clear without turning every message into an assignment.
- In an active thread, answer the latest question and add only missing context. For a new thread, email, or forwarded message, include enough context to stand alone.
- For a request, make the decision or action answerable. Include an owner, deadline, and reason for urgency when known and relevant; do not invent them.
- For an update, give space to consequential changes and blockers. Compress routine progress rather than reporting every activity equally.
- When several people receive a request, identify who needs to respond if that is known. Avoid leaving everyone to assume someone else will act.
- Use an email subject that identifies the topic and, when useful, the requested action. In chat, make the opening line useful in a notification preview.
- For difficult news, state what happened, acknowledge its effect, and explain the next step within the writer's control. Keep enough warmth to avoid sounding dismissive; do not bury the news in reassurance or promise an outcome the writer cannot guarantee.

## Example

Vague: “Quick update: we've been looking at the export issue and would appreciate everyone's thoughts on the best path forward.”

Answerable: “The export still drops rows. Priya, can you confirm by 2 pm whether we should pause today's release? My recommendation is to pause until the fix is verified.”

Use those details only when supplied or verified. Before returning, check whether the recipient can respond without asking what the writer wants.
