# Human Writing

Write for a smart, busy reader. Express each idea in its simplest natural form, and preserve the author's meaning, uncertainty, and voice.

## Choose the approach

Infer who is reading, what they already know, and what the writing should help them understand, decide, do, or experience. Ask a focused question only when missing information could materially change the result.

Read the one reference that matches the reader's task. Read a second only when the deliverable genuinely combines tasks.

- [Messages](references/messages.md): correspondence, including Slack and email, where someone needs to respond or stay informed.
- [Documents](references/documents.md): standalone explanations, proposals, guides, articles, and blogs.
- [Overviews](references/overviews.md): summaries, handovers, and introductions to work, including PR descriptions.
- [Review comments](references/review-comments.md): feedback attached to a document, design, or code change.
- [Product copy](references/product-copy.md): labels, instructions, feedback, and other text within an interface.

When a specialist skill applies, let it own the workflow, required content, and format. Apply this skill to the prose inside that format.

## Draft

- For work communication, usually lead with the outcome, decision, request, change, or problem. Choose a different opening when learning, narrative, or a sensitive conversation needs it.
- Prefer simple words and sentence structures. Remove unnecessary ideas before tightening the wording.
- Keep distinct ideas in separate sentences when combining them would make the writing denser or harder to follow.
- Prefer concrete language. Include names, dates, owners, and numbers when they aid understanding or action.
- Make requests and next steps explicit, but do not manufacture an action for an informative message.
- Say only what serves the communication's purpose. Omit background, examples, qualifications, and side points unless they change understanding, confidence, a decision, or an action.
- Preserve meaningful uncertainty, disagreement, and trade-offs. Do not invent facts or consensus.
- Order ideas for the reader's task: importance for an update, sequence for instructions, or a developing argument for an essay. Use paragraphs by default and structure only when it improves navigation or scanning.
- Match the reader's knowledge, the relationship, the writer's vocabulary, and the conventions of the medium.
- Include only the sensitive detail the reader needs.

## Sound Human

Be direct without being abrupt. Use warmth to make the message easier to receive, not to pad it. Assume good intent, especially in disagreement, and leave room to respond or decline.

Remove:

- throat-clearing, generic openings, and unnecessary recaps
- mannered prose, corporate filler, marketing fluff, and management clichés
- canned praise, performative agreement, or forced enthusiasm
- manufactured contrasts, rhetorical questions, and dramatic fragments
- redundant restatement and lists created for symmetry
- vague hedging that hides the actual view
- excessive headings, bolding, or bullets

Prefer one natural sentence over several slogan-like fragments.

## Final Check

Check whether the intended reader has enough context to achieve the purpose and whether any necessary action is unmistakable. Then look for details to drop: most drafts carry some that don't serve the purpose. Keep the explanation, evidence, or completeness the task needs. Do not make a sentence denser merely to reduce the word count. Return the finished copy without explaining the edits unless asked.
