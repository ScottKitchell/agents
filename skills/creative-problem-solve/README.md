# creative-problem-solve

Finds non-obvious solutions by mapping the problem space, abstracting it to a domain-free core structure, raiding many other domains that share that structure, and returning only the ideas that survive harsh judgement.

## Why it exists

Most stuck problems are stuck because of the frame, not a lack of effort: everyone in a domain reaches for the same solutions. Agents make this worse, because early ideas anchor later ones and they rediscover the obvious. Other domains have often solved the same structure in ways nobody here has considered.

Design choices:

- **Write down "the box"** (the obvious solutions) so later ideas can be checked for real novelty.
- **Generate analogies category by category**, returning to the core structure each time, to counter fixation.
- **Aim for a mix of analogical distances, weighted to the middle.** Design-by-analogy studies find far analogies raise novelty while near ones keep quality and feasibility, with a moderate distance often the best trade-off ([Chan et al. 2011](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chanetal2011.pdf); [Fu et al. 2013](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/fuchanetal-near-far-2013.pdf)). Chan, Dow and Schunn (2015) dispute that far sources produce the best ideas, so the skill doesn't chase maximum weirdness.
- **Prefer shared relationships over surface features**, and extract the transferable mechanism rather than the observation.
- **Look for convergence**: a mechanism several unrelated domains arrived at independently is probably robust.

`expert-opinion` borrows from other domains briefly to settle a decision; this skill is for a broad hunt for new ideas.

## Iterating

- Test with a small, medium, and large problem and check the depth scales: analogy count, research effort, and number of ideas returned.
- Check the response leads with ranked ideas and their analogies, and keeps the 5 Whys tree and full analogy list out unless asked.
